## The prompt cache after a pause

Share of calls that read at least 90% of the previous context from cache, by the pause before the call (contexts over 4k tokens; compactions excluded):

| Pause | claude-code | codex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| under 60 s | 99% (259,443) | 97% (327,248) |
| 60 s–5 min | 98% (26,345) | 86% (17,014) |
| 5 min–10 min | 48% (6,134) | 73% (3,347) |
| 10 min–30 min | 51% (3,828) | 62% (2,962) |
| 30 min–60 min | 63% (1,446) | 37% (880) |
| over 60 min | 1.4% (1,911) | 8.5% (1,231) |

Claude Code's hit rate falls by about half once the pause passes five minutes (the default cache lifetime) and to almost nothing after an hour; Codex decays more gradually. That is what the cache-expired check measures: pauses where the context had to be prefilled again. Whether all of it is avoidable depends on what keeping the cache costs: a one-hour cache write is priced at 2× input against 1.25× for five minutes, which pays back on the first re-read after a pause of 5–60 minutes.

## How much the thresholds matter

With a one-hour cache lifetime instead of five minutes, and 20k tokens instead of 8k as the oversized-output threshold, the avoidable share is 21% (vs 22%): most of what was "expired" is then counted as plain cache misses, and fewer tool results count as oversized. The total barely moves; which label it gets depends on what you assume the provider's cache lifetime is.

## What this trace can't show

- TraceLab records tool names and result sizes but not arguments, so repeated tool calls can't be detected.
- It doesn't record the tool definitions sent with each call (unused tools) or task outcomes (cost per successful task); see VALIDATION-SWEBENCH.md for outcomes.
